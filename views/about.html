About

Every calendar day has a past. This site lets you turn the dial and read it.

How to use it

  • Drag the outer ring of the dial to change the day, the middle disc to change the month, or the blue centre to scrub through the whole year.
  • Prefer controls? Use the month and day menus, the arrows, or Today. On a keyboard, ← and → step one day, and / jumps to search.
  • Switch between events, births, deaths and holidays, search within the day, and flip the order from oldest to newest.
  • The address always holds your date and category, so you can copy it and share exactly what you are looking at.

Where the history comes from

All historical entries come live from Wikipedia's On This Day feed. Nothing is written or edited here. Text is available under CC BY-SA 4.0, and every entry links back to the Wikipedia article it came from. The server keeps a copy of each day for a few hours so the page loads fast and Wikipedia is not asked the same question twice.

Notes, the Wall and your privacy

If you add a note on Add a note, we store exactly what you type: the note (up to 2000 characters), the date you chose, an optional display name, an optional photo, and the time you posted it. Public notes appear on the Wall and on their own page. Private notes are visible only to you, when you are logged in.

  • Photos are shrunk in your browser before upload, which also removes camera data such as GPS location. One photo per note.
  • Accounts are optional. If you make one we store a username and a hashed password (never the password itself). Logging in sets one cookie that keeps you signed in for 30 days; it is not used for tracking. There is no email and no analytics.
  • Guests get a secret delete key when they post. It is kept in your browser, and we only store a hash of it. Clear your browser data and you lose the ability to delete that note yourself.
  • Spam limits: to allow 10 notes per hour, we keep a scrambled (hashed) form of your network address and your account for about an hour, then it is deleted automatically. We cannot turn it back into an IP address.
  • Please do not post anything you would not want public. Public notes with links are rejected.
  • Logged-in users can delete their own notes any time from My notes. To have another note removed, contact the site owner through GitHub with the note ID.
